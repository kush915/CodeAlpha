1. What is phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack where criminals pretend to be someone you trust (bank, boss, delivery company, government) to trick you into clicking a link, opening a file, or giving away passwords, OTPs or money. Over 90% of successful breaches start with a human action, not a technical hack. That is why awareness is the strongest defence.
The goal
Steal credentials, install malware, commit payment fraud, or get a foothold in a company.
The method
Create urgency, fear or greed so you act before you think.
The defence
Pause. Verify through another channel. Report.

2. Types of phishing

Email
Mass phishing
Generic emails sent to thousands: "Your account is suspended."
Targeted
Spear phishing
Personalised using your name, job and colleagues found on LinkedIn.
Executive
Whaling / BEC
Fake CEO or vendor asks finance to transfer money urgently.
SMS
Smishing
Text: "Your parcel is held, pay Rs 25 fee" with a short link.
Phone
Vishing
Caller poses as bank/IT support and asks for OTP or remote access.
QR code
Quishing
Malicious QR code on a poster or email that leads to a fake login.
Web
Clone sites
Copy of a real login page on a look-alike domain that captures what you type.
Social
Social media
Fake job offers, giveaways, support accounts and "verify your page" DMs.

4. Fake websites and URLs

The only part that matters is the registered domain, which is the text right before the first single /, read from the right side of the name.

https://mybank.com/login  real (fictional)
https://mybank.verify-login.example/login  fake: real domain is verify-login.example
https://rnybank.com/login  fake: "rn" looks like "m"
https://mybank.com@evil.example/login  fake: everything before @ is ignored
https://mybank-support-24x7.example  fake: extra words and odd extension
Website checklist
  • Check spelling and the exact domain, not just the padlock (criminals also get HTTPS certificates).
  • Don't follow links from messages: open the app or type the address yourself.
  • Be suspicious of pages that ask for card number + PIN + OTP all at once.
  • Hover (or long-press) on links to preview the destination. Use URLScan.io or VirusTotal to check unknown links safely.

5. Social engineering: the psychology

Urgency
"Act in 1 hour or lose access." Time pressure switches off careful thinking.
Authority
Posing as the CEO, police, RBI, IT help desk. People obey titles.
Fear
"Your account is hacked", "legal action will be taken".
Greed / reward
Lottery wins, cashback, free gifts, easy jobs.
Curiosity
"Is this you in the video?" or a USB stick left in the parking lot.
Trust and helpfulness
"Hi, I'm new, can you help me with access?" Kindness gets exploited.
Golden rule: if a message makes you feel rushed, scared or excited, stop. That feeling is the attack.

6. Real-world examples

2011 RSA breach
Employees opened an email titled "2011 Recruitment plan" with an Excel attachment. It installed malware that led to theft of data related to RSA's SecurID products.
2013-2015 Fake invoice fraud on tech giants
A scammer sent fake invoices from a spoofed hardware vendor and tricked accounts-payable teams at two large tech companies into paying over $100 million. Most of it was later recovered.
2016 US election email compromise
A fake "security alert" with a link to change a password led to the compromise of a campaign chairman's mailbox. One click, huge consequences.
2020 Twitter employee vishing
Attackers phoned employees pretending to be internal IT, got access to admin tools and took over high-profile accounts for a crypto scam.

Lesson: even tech-savvy organisations fall for it, so everyone needs the habit of verifying.

7. Best practices

Before you click
  • Check the real sender address.
  • Hover over links.
  • Ask: was I expecting this?
  • Never open unexpected attachments.
Protect accounts
  • Enable MFA / authenticator app everywhere.
  • Use a password manager (it won't autofill on fake sites).
  • Unique password per site.
  • Never share OTPs, PINs or CVV.
Verify
  • Call the official number from the website or card.
  • Confirm money requests on a second channel.
  • Navigate to sites yourself.
If you clicked
  • Disconnect from the network if you opened a file.
  • Change the password and enable MFA.
  • Tell IT / your bank immediately.
  • India: report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
